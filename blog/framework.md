  <p class="subtitle">A framework for how I operate.</p>

<article class="prose" markdown="1">

When something needs to change, you can start working on it before you know everything required to finish.

People rarely wake up wanting to acquire knowledge for its own sake. They wake up with a doubt, a problem, an outcome they want, or a desire to improve their circumstances. Learning follows from pursuing that change.

## Build judgment

Judgment is knowing what matters, what is worth pursuing, what to ignore, and how to approach it.

I developed much of mine through coding. Implementing a solution exposed missing pieces and assumptions I hadn’t considered. The manual work created time to think, encounter consequences, and revise my understanding.

As AI takes on more of the implementation, that process needs to become deliberate. More thought has to go into framing the problem, questioning assumptions, and deciding what a good result looks like. Some judgment still develops through the attempt itself: examining the output, finding what breaks, and understanding why.

The temptation, especially when entering an unfamiliar field, is to believe you need more expertise before you can begin. I have often responded by studying existing implementations, inheriting their authors’ abstractions before trusting myself to develop my own.

That can help, but it can also become a way of postponing the work.

Access to powerful tools does not automatically produce powerful work. Two people can have the same models and capabilities and produce entirely different results. The difference includes what they notice, what they care enough to pursue, and their ability to recognize quality.

**Build judgment by making decisions and staying close enough to their consequences to learn from them.**

## Make things happen

Pick a real problem and cause a change in the world. Learn whatever you need along the way.

Expertise often develops through the effort to reach an outcome. The problem gives learning direction: it reveals which knowledge matters, where understanding is incomplete, and whether an approach actually works.

Studying a domain and its tools can prepare you, but progress requires applying that knowledge to something you want to change. The attempt exposes questions you would not have known to ask beforehand.

This also shapes how I think about careers. Optimizing for a job can become optimizing for being tested. I want to develop the ability to build and deliver, an ability that remains useful across different jobs and circumstances.

**Treat a meaningful change in the world as the measure of progress.**

## Take bigger bets

Pursue uncertain opportunities where the upside matters and the cost of failure is something you can afford.

Ambitious work will put you in situations where you do not yet have the necessary capability. Some attempts will fail. Giving yourself room for that failure is part of becoming capable of more.

A concrete outcome forces you to engage with the actual problem, make decisions, and learn from what happens. Experience helps, but requiring it before every attempt limits you to things you already know how to do.

The judgment lies in choosing the bet: understanding why it matters, what you are risking, and what would justify continuing or stopping.

**You need enough confidence to begin without requiring certainty that you will succeed.**

## Make others care

Useful work needs people who understand its value. Learn to articulate why something matters, earn trust, and persuade others to use it, join it, fund it, or support it.

Delivering outcomes builds that trust. Your ideas carry more weight when people have evidence that you can follow through.

The same principle informs how I think about content. The pursuit itself should produce stories worth telling:

> What is the most interesting thing that happened while doing this?

A surprising discovery, a failed assumption, a difficult decision, or a visible result can all become something worth sharing.

But the medium matters. A good story still needs to be expressed in a form people can engage with. Short-form video often requires something visually compelling. The value of the work has to become visible; sharing a conversation link rarely achieves that on its own.

**Do work that gives you something to say, then learn how to make its significance clear.**

## Build what compounds

Put effort into things whose value stays with you when your circumstances change.

Judgment, craft, relationships, reputation, financial resources, and work you own can continue to create value beyond a particular job or institution.

A useful question when choosing what to pursue is:

> What remains valuable when this opportunity ends?

A job can leave you with stronger capabilities, trusted relationships, and evidence of what you can accomplish. A project can produce something useful today while making future work easier or more ambitious.

**Look for work whose benefits accumulate beyond the immediate outcome.**
